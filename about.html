About me

Dundee and Africa

I study Computer Science with Data Science and AI in Scotland, and I build software for markets across Africa, starting with South Sudan.

Who I am

I'm Lam, a second-year BSc Computer Science (Data Science and AI) student at the University of Dundee. I grew up with strong ties to Juba, South Sudan. That is where most of my products start, but the goal is markets across Africa.

That focus started with a simple observation: software built elsewhere tends to assume card payments, reliable street addresses and strong connectivity. In Juba none of those are safe assumptions. Designing around them is the most interesting engineering problem I know.

I work under my own brand, Zatzi, across architecture, backend, mobile and the product and brand decisions around them. Alongside that I'm co-developing Nylo, a learning platform for public schools, with a friend.

Reflection

What building for real users has taught me

The hard problems were not the code

When I started on Zatride I expected the difficult part to be real-time dispatch. It wasn't. The difficult part was that riders in Juba describe where they are by landmark, pay with MTN or Zain Cash rather than cards, and choose between a car, a bodaboda and a tuk-tuk on price. Each of those changed the data model before it changed a single screen. I now start every project by listing the assumptions the product cannot make.

Technology that has to be responsible

Nylo will hold data about children, so the design starts from collecting as little as possible and giving each role (student, parent, teacher, Ministry) access only to what it needs. Its AI assistant answers children's questions, which means wrong or unsuitable answers are a safety issue, not a quality issue. Zatride handles money and live location, where reliability is what earns trust. Accessibility matters in a different way here too: low-cost Android phones and patchy connectivity are the normal case, not an edge case.

Where I'm weaker, and what I'm doing about it

My skills audit for this module showed gaps in enterprise databases (SQL Server, Azure), in automated testing, and in measuring outcomes with numbers rather than describing them. Working in a group of four on the Wordle AI Solver also taught me that code other people can read matters more than code I find elegant, so I now comment and structure my code for the least experienced person on the team.

What's next

Over the next year I want to secure a 2027 placement in software, data or AI, complete a cloud data certification to close the database gap, and get one product in front of real users with measured results I can report.
