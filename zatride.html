Zatride

Ride-hailing designed around how Juba actually moves: landmarks instead of addresses, mobile money instead of cards, and bodabodas alongside cars.

Overview

Zatride is a ride-hailing platform for Juba covering the three ways people actually get around the city: cars, bodabodas (motorbike taxis) and tuk-tuks. It has a rider app, a driver app and an admin dashboard on a shared real-time backend.

The problem

International ride-hailing apps assume things that don't hold in Juba. Street addresses are not how people describe where they are. Most people pay with mobile money, not cards. And a bodaboda and a car are different products at very different prices, not one category.

Design decisions

  • Landmark-based pickup. Riders choose a known landmark rather than typing an address, because that is how directions are given in Juba.
  • Mobile money. Payment through MTN and Zain Cash, with pricing in South Sudanese pounds (SSP).
  • Three vehicle types. Cars, bodabodas and tuk-tuks are priced and dispatched separately.
  • Real-time by default. Socket.io carries trip state between rider, driver and dashboard, with Redis holding live state so the database is not hit on every update.

How I built it

I defined the architecture, the data model and the full screen inventory, then used AI coding tools (Claude Code) to generate and iterate the implementation. My job was the decisions an AI tool can't make: what the product must assume, how trips move through their states, and reviewing what was produced.

Status and next steps

A full prototype and backend are built. Next: field testing with drivers in Juba, and integrating the live MTN and Zain Cash payment APIs.
